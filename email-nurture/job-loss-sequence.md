## Email 2: Day 2
**Subject:** The first 30 days after a layoff
**Preview text:** What actually needs your attention right now

Hi {{first_name}},

If you've had a chance to open the workbook, you've probably seen the "First 30 Days Checkpoint" section. A few things worth repeating here:

- **Confirm your severance details**: including how and when it's paid, and whether it affects unemployment eligibility.
- **Know your benefits deadlines**: especially health coverage. COBRA elections typically have a 60-day window.
- **Avoid big financial decisions right now.** Nothing about your 401(k), your home, or a major purchase needs to be decided this week.

The goal of the first 30 days isn't to have a plan. It's to stabilize.

If you want a second set of eyes on your severance package or benefits timeline, that's exactly what a Where We Begin session is for.

## Email 3: Day 5
**Subject:** What to do with your 401(k) (and what not to do)
**Preview text:** This decision is easy to get wrong under time pressure

Hi {{first_name}},

One question I hear constantly after a layoff: "What do I do with my old 401(k)?"

The honest answer: it depends, and it's rarely urgent. Generally you have a few paths, leave it where it is, roll it into an IRA, or (in some cases) roll it into a new employer's plan later. Each has different cost, investment option, and tax implications.

What I'd avoid: cashing it out. Between taxes and an early withdrawal penalty, it's usually the most expensive option, even when it's tempting during a cash crunch.

If you're staring at this decision and not sure which way to go, let's talk it through. No cost, no obligation.
