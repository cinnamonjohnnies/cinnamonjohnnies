# Outbound Prospecting Strategy: Referral Partners, Not Individuals

## Why this approach, not direct-to-consumer list-buying

A tempting version of "outbound prospecting" for this niche would be to scrape or buy lists of individuals currently going through a layoff, divorce filing, or bereavement, and cold-contact them directly. **This document deliberately does not do that**, for three reasons:

1. **Regulatory exposure.** Checkpoint Planning operates under FINRA/SEC rules (via Ameritas Investment Company / Ameritas Advisory Services) governing advertising, solicitation, and the use of personal data. Unsolicited outreach to someone using scraped personal-hardship data (a layoff, a divorce filing, a death record) is the kind of practice that draws regulatory and reputational scrutiny, even where technically legal in a given state.
2. **It's a bad first impression for a trust-based practice.** Checkpoint Planning's entire positioning, fiduciary, empathetic, "we ask the right questions," "no agenda", is undermined by contacting someone out of the blue about the worst week of their life.
3. **It's not actually how this niche gets clients.** Financial planners who specialize in life-transition work build their pipeline almost entirely through **centers of influence (COIs)**: the professionals who are already in the room with someone during that transition, before they think to look for a financial planner.

## The compliant, higher-converting alternative: COI referral partnerships

Build relationships with professionals who meet your ideal client *before* you do:

| Transition | Referral partner types |
|---|---|
| Job loss / layoff | Outplacement firms, career coaches, employment attorneys, HR consultants at mid-size companies |
| Divorce | Divorce attorneys, family law mediators, divorce coaches, therapists specializing in divorce |
| Early retirement | CPAs, HR/benefits consultants, employee benefits brokers |
| Inheritance | Estate planning attorneys, trust officers, probate attorneys, CPAs |

Every one of these categories already maps to a specialty area Scott coordinates on for existing clients (estate/trust, tax, business exit), you already operate on a referral-partner model. This extends it to net-new partners. Note: per compliance guidance, none of these referral partners should be named on the website or in client-facing marketing material unless they are affiliated with Ameritas; keep names in an internal contact list only.

### What "prospecting" means in this context

Instead of a list of consumers, build a **target list of local professionals** (Orange County / Southern California first, since in-person coffee/lunch meetings convert far better than cold email, expand later to other markets where you have existing client density).

Use Clay or Vibe Prospecting to build and enrich a list of:
- Family law / divorce attorneys with solo or small-firm practices in Orange County
- Estate planning / probate attorneys in Orange County
- CPAs and enrolled agents in Orange County who don't offer investment management themselves (they're not competitors, they're a referral pipeline into your business, and you're a referral pipeline into theirs)
- Outplacement and career coaching firms serving Southern California employers
- HR/benefits brokers serving mid-size Orange County employers (early retirement + layoff overlap)

### Suggested Clay / Vibe Prospecting search criteria

```
Industry:       Legal services (family law), Legal services (estate planning/probate),
                Accounting (tax/CPA), HR consulting, Career coaching/outplacement
Geography:      Orange County, CA (primary), Los Angeles / San Diego (secondary)
Company size:   1-25 employees (solo practitioners and small firms refer more
                readily and personally than large firms with existing referral networks)
Title:          Owner, Partner, Principal, Managing Attorney, Managing Partner
Exclude:        Firms that already offer in-house wealth management or financial
                planning (they're competitors, not partners)
```

Once you have a list, enrich each contact with: firm name, direct email, LinkedIn URL, and firm website, everything needed for a personalized outreach note referencing something specific about their practice, not a mail-merge blast.

### Outreach approach (not cold, warm-by-design)

1. **Don't lead with a pitch.** Lead with the workbook/guide relevant to their clients (e.g., send the Divorce Financial Checkpoint Guide to a divorce attorney as something *they* can hand to *their* clients, with your name on it).
2. **Offer value first.** "I built this guide for my own clients going through divorce, thought it might be useful for yours too. Happy to be a resource if financial questions come up in your cases."
3. **Meet in person when possible.** A 20-minute coffee with 3-5 target partners a month compounds far faster than any cold outreach volume.
4. **Track referral source on every lead.** The website lead form already captures `source_page`, extend this practice offline by asking new clients "who referred you?" and logging it in the same Leads sheet (add a row manually, or extend the intake form) so you can see which partnerships are actually producing.
